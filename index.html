Student Vectors

We have the list of all students' courses, from term 1 until now. We want to find features from them so that, in a vector database, the closer students can be clustered as friends.

I want to solve this question two times with the same data: once for finding friends, and once for finding the field of study — now, or in the future. The fields are seven: Communications, RF, Power, Electronics, Bio, Digital and Control.

I really believed this is graph-structured data, but for my reasons I prefer not to use a graph and go with just vectors. At the end I want to have a vector database.

Synthetic data only. Every chart here uses 600 generated students with known friends and fields. No real student records.

My sketch

Each course is one axis. Every group of that course is mapped to a number on it.

Theory of Electric Circuits has groups g1, g2, g3 … g6, and each one goes to a number on the x₁ axis. Eng Math does the same on x₂. Two students who were in the same groups end up in the same cell of the grid.

Some cells are interference: the two classes are at the same time, so nobody can take both.

Groups → numbers

The point is mapping groups to numbers so that the classes which have interference stay between the clusters — then the clustering is much easier.

Two courses, six groups each

Each dot is a student. Hatched cells are time clashes: nobody can take both. Hover a cell for details.

Group numbering:

Which courses

We just use the courses with a large number of groups, like 5 or 6, that people can choose from. These courses are almost mandatory, so nearly everyone has a value. By this act we also shrink the dimension of the space.

Students who didn't take a course

We use a group for the people who don't take the course: group NULL.

● Open

Known bug: the NULL group

We have a bug on the NULL group, and we haven't solved it yet.

Everyone who didn't take a course gets the same group, NULL. So two students who both skipped a course look as if they sat in the same class, and they get pulled together — they can look like friends without ever having met. Because NULL is rare in the almost-mandatory courses, it can also get a bigger weight than a normal group, and the NULL students can form a cluster of their own.

Status: open. If you have an idea for fixing it, it's welcome.

How the numbers are computed

No graph: the numbers come straight from the table of students × chosen groups, with Multiple Correspondence Analysis. Groups that the same students choose get close numbers; groups that are never chosen together, like interfering classes, get pushed apart.

The 7D space

To find the students' field we use a 7D space.

We consider the students of each class who finally chose their field. Each class gets a 7-dimensional vector whose numbers add up to 1. This vector is our prior: the probability that a student of that class goes to each field in the future.

Its geometric space is a strange, undesirable shape — in higher dimensions we call it the simplex (|x₁| + |x₂| + … + |x₇| = 1).

Because this shape is ugly, we use P′ = { √P : P ∈ Simplex } to reach the sphere x₁² + x₂² + … + x₇² = 1. Since all probabilities are positive, we only work with 1/2⁷ of it.

Students who haven't chosen a field yet are not counted in the class priors. Every term, when students choose their fields, the priors are updated.

Simplex → sphere (drawn with 2 fields)

With two fields the simplex is the line x₁ + x₂ = 1 and the sphere is the circle x₁² + x₂² = 1. Move the two classes.

Updating like a GPA

Every student, after enrolling in each course, gets an updated field attitude — just like a GPA.

Some facts

  1. All the data vectors are positive.
  2. Using L2 normalization (the unit circle) means only the direction matters, and we don't need to worry about the size.

A drawback

We don't use the whole space: our database moves on the shell of the ball B⁷, which is S⁷⁻¹ = S⁶. So our vectors really come from a 6-dimensional space — which makes sense, because we know the squares of the probabilities must add up to 1.

Method

Each class of the student ↦ vi ∈ S⁶ (the unit sphere in 7D). The student's field attitude is

vt = Σi=1..n vi / ‖ Σi=1..n vi ‖2

A student's field attitude, course by course

Add the classes a student takes. Each one moves the attitude, like a GPA. Class priors come from the synthetic data.

Recommending

Once the students are in the vector database, the same two vectors can recommend things.

  • Friends and study partners — the nearest students on the friends vector.
  • Electives — classes whose prior is close to the student's field vector, and classes the student's friends took.
  • Which group to pick — the group the student's friends are in.
  • Field — the student's 7 numbers, now or for the future.

Results on synthetic data

The fake data knows the real friends and fields, so we can check the methods.

≈ 44%of each student's 3 nearest neighbours are real friends (random ≈ 0.6%)
≈ 47%correct field for held-out students who chose a field (random 14%)
≈ 59%correct field in the top 2

Setup: 600 students in 4 entry years, friend groups of 3–5 who choose the same group 80% of the time, and electives that depend on the field. Before a student takes any elective, the field can't be told yet.
