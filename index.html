Student Vectors

Turning course enrollments into two vectors per student — one that puts friends close together, one that points toward a field of study — and using a vector database to build a recommender system on top.

Synthetic data only. Every chart and number in these docs comes from 600 generated students with known friend groups and fields, so the methods can be checked against the truth. No real student records are shown.

Idea in one paragraph

From the first term until now, each student leaves a trail: which courses, in which term, in which group (section). Friends keep registering in the same group of big multi-group courses; future fields show up in which electives a student picks. We encode the first as coordinates from Multiple Correspondence Analysis, and the second as a 7-dimensional probability vector mapped onto the unit sphere by a square root. Both are stored per student in one vector database, where every question becomes a nearest-neighbour query.

The pipeline

Two independent representations from the same enrollment table, stored side by side.

Friend vectors

Map each group to a number so that friends share cells and impossible combinations become the gaps between clusters.

Big courses like Circuits or Engineering Math run in 5–6 parallel groups. Friends tend to register in the same group of the same course in the same term — again and again. Give each course an axis and each group a position on it, and friends land in the same cell.

The trick is which number each group gets. Some group pairs are impossible because their class times clash (“interference”). If we number the groups well, those empty cells fall between the clusters instead of in the middle of them, and clustering becomes easy. Correspondence analysis (the 2-course case of MCA) finds that numbering directly from the counts.

Two courses, six groups each

Each dot is a student. Hatched cells are time clashes: nobody can take both. Hover a cell for details.

Group numbering:

With many courses you don't look at a grid any more: MCA does the same thing for all selected courses at once and gives each student a 16-dimensional vector. Courses a student never took are a NULL value, so there are no empty slots. In the vector database, “who are this student's friends?” becomes a nearest-neighbour query.

Field-of-study vectors

A 7D attitude per student, built from course priors and updated after every course, like a GPA.

Seven fields: Communications, RF, Power, Electronics, Bio, Digital, Control. For every course we look at the students who later declared a field and count where they went. That gives each course a 7-number prior that sums to 1 — a point on the simplex. Every term, newly declared students update these priors.

Taking the square root of each probability moves the point onto the unit sphere S⁶ ⊂ ℝ⁷, in its positive part (1/2⁷ of it). On the sphere, the dot product of two vectors is the Bhattacharyya coefficient Σ√(pᵢqᵢ), so cosine similarity in the vector database has a probabilistic meaning.

Simplex → sphere, shown with 2 fields

With two fields, the simplex is the segment x₁ + x₂ = 1 and the sphere is the quarter circle x₁² + x₂² = 1. Move the two courses and watch what averaging does.

Course A, share of field 1   Course B

The average of √p vectors lies on the chord, inside the circle: its length is , not 1. Dividing by its length puts it back on the sphere:

vt = Σ vi / ‖Σ vi‖2

Divide by the norm, not the squared norm — ‖·‖₂² would give length 1/‖Σvᵢ‖.

The normalized point is close to, but not the same as, √(average p): angle between them . Pick one rule and use it everywhere.

course vectors √p raw average (inside) normalized average √(average p)

Mandatory courses drown out the signal

Everyone takes Circuits, so its prior is just the overall field mix — it says nothing about this student. With a plain or normalized average, 30 mandatory courses bury the 2 electives that actually tell you something. The fix is to add up how much each course shifts the odds compared to the overall mix:

liftc(f) = log( pc(f) / π(f) )    s(f) = log π(f) + α Σc wc liftc(f)    P = softmax(s),   v = √P

Mandatory courses get lift ≈ 0 and add nothing; telling electives move the score a lot. Each finished course just adds its term — the same running update as a GPA.

The vector database

One point per student, two named vectors, a payload for filtering.

Each student is one point with two named vectors, plus a payload of plain fields for filtering. Both vectors are unit length, so the database uses cosine distance for both.

One point per student

PartSizeMeaning
friends16DMCA coordinates of the student's group choices
field7D√P — field attitude on the unit sphere
payload—student id, entry year, predicted / declared field, friend group

Not concatenated on purpose: each question uses its own vector, and the two scales never mix.

Queries

client.create_collection("students", vectors_config={
    "friends": VectorParams(size=16, distance=Distance.COSINE),
    "field":   VectorParams(size=7,  distance=Distance.COSINE),
})

# likely friends
client.query_points("students", query=v_friends,
                    using="friends", limit=6)

# students heading the same way, same entry year only

    query_filter=Filter(must=[FieldCondition(

Recommender systems

Who is similar to whom, and what fits whom: the two vectors answer both.

A recommender system needs two things: a way to say who is similar to whom, and a way to say what fits whom. The two vectors give exactly that.

Collaborative

Study partners & friends

Nearest neighbours on the friends vector. “Students who keep choosing the same groups as you.” Useful for study groups and project teams.

Content-based

Electives that fit your field

Score each elective by the dot product of the student's field vector with the course's √prior. On the sphere this is the overlap of two distributions.

Hybrid

Mix both

Score = λ · field fit + (1 − λ) · share of your nearest friends who took it. λ = 1 is pure content; λ = 0 is pure “your friends did it”.

Registration

Which group to pick

For a course with 6 groups, suggest the group most of your nearest neighbours registered in — skipping groups that clash with your timetable.

Advising

Field guidance

Show the 7 probabilities and which courses moved them most (largest lift). An explanation, not a verdict.

Evaluation on synthetic data

The generator knows every student's real friend group and field, so the methods can be scored exactly.

Setup

600 students in 4 entry years, split into friend groups of 3–5. Friends pick the same group number in each big course 80% of the time. Each student takes their own field's electives with probability 0.6 and other fields' with 0.06, and declares a field in semester 7.

Friends

Field of study

≈ 47%correct field on held-out declared students (random: 14%)

chancefor students with no electives yet — expected, see below
Why chance level for early students? Mandatory courses have lift ≈ 0 by design, so a student who has not picked any electives stays at the overall mix. The model only becomes confident once there is information to be confident about.

Testing without leakage

Course priors are learned only from students who declared before the test term, and test students' courses are cut at their declaration term. α is chosen on that hold-out by log-loss.

What this project combines

Not claimed as a new method. The combination is: working at the level of the group (section) rather than the course; using time-clash structure so that correspondence analysis places impossible combinations between clusters; a GPA-style lift update of field probabilities stored on the Hellinger sphere; and both representations side by side in one vector database serving several recommendation tasks.

Limits & ethics

What the vectors cannot tell you, and how real data should be handled.

Same schedule ≠ friends

Cohort, major and timetable constraints also produce shared groups. Check with a few known friend pairs (precision@k) before trusting clusters.

No electives, no signal

Before a student picks electives, the field vector stays near the overall mix. That's honest: the data doesn't know yet.

Leakage

When testing, build course priors only from students who declared before the one being predicted.

Privacy

Inferring friendships from enrollment records is sensitive. Real data should be anonymized, used with consent, and kept out of public pages like this one.
